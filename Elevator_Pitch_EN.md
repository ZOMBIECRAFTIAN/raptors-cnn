# Elevator pitch in English - 90 seconds

Three versions for three situations. Pick the one that matches the time you have. **Memorise verbatim.** Practice five times out loud before Friday.

---

## Version 1 - 30 seconds (the hallway)

> "Hi, I'm Brian Fernandez. I'm a master's candidate from Mexico. My research program is **multi-modal AI for biodiversity identification with accessibility built in**. I have three open-source projects on GitHub: a visual raptor identifier for Australia with sign-language vocabulary, a scaled version for the 53 raptors of Mexico that adds flight-behaviour analysis, and a passive acoustic monitoring system for mammals, amphibians, reptiles and insects. The doctorate I want to pursue is the fusion of all three: **vision plus audio plus sign language for continent-scale fauna monitoring** that is accessible to the Deaf community."

**Word count:** ~110. **Speaking time:** ~30 seconds at 220 wpm. Use if the professor asks "tell me about yourself" in the elevator or at coffee before the meeting.

---

## Version 2 - 90 seconds (the official slot)

> "Good morning, thank you for taking the time. My name is Brian Fernandez Baez. I'm a master's candidate from Mexico.
>
> Over the last year I have built three open-source projects that together define a research program. The first is an **Australian raptor identifier** with eight species, an EfficientNet-B4 model that achieves F1-macro 0.85, and a proposed AUSLAN sign vocabulary - this was the foundation for an MPhil proposal at the University of Queensland.
>
> The second is my master's thesis: **raptors of Mexico** scaled to 53 species across all diurnal raptors, benchmarking four CNN architectures, with two key novelties. We train on the **silhouette in flight**, not on plumage, which is how an ornithologist actually decides in the field. And we add a **Bayesian behaviour module** that fuses static visual classification with flight-mode analysis from short video. The sign-language layer is in **International Sign**, broader than AUSLAN, with 53 signs co-designed with the Deaf community.
>
> The third project is **passive acoustic monitoring** for mammals, amphibians, reptiles, and insects. Production stack: PyTorch with PANNs pre-trained on AudioSet, FastAPI, PostgreSQL with PostGIS, Docker compose, CI/CD. Version 0.3 already runs end-to-end.
>
> The natural doctorate is the fusion: **vision plus audio plus sign language** for continent-scale, accessible biodiversity monitoring. That's why I asked for this conversation."

**Word count:** ~280. **Speaking time:** ~90 seconds at 190 wpm. Use this as your opener if the professor invites you to start with "tell me about your work."

## Version 3 - 3 minutes (full intro before slides)

Use if the meeting is informal and there's no slide deck slot for slide 1. Otherwise just open the deck.

> "Thank you for making time, Professor. My name is Brian Fernandez Baez. I'm finishing a master's in Mexico and looking at where to do my doctorate.
>
> I want to spend just three minutes giving you the big picture before we go into the slides.
>
> My research program is **multi-modal artificial intelligence for biodiversity identification, with accessibility designed in from day one**. That sentence has three load-bearing pieces. **Multi-modal** because I work with both vision and bioacoustics. **Biodiversity** because I focus on real ecological problems - fire recovery monitoring in Australia, the world's largest raptor migration corridor in Mexico, and passive acoustic monitoring of fauna in general. And **accessibility** because every project ships with a signed-language vocabulary co-designed with the Deaf community.
>
> I have three live projects.
>
> Project one is the **Australian Raptor CNN**, eight species, EfficientNet-B4. The model reaches F1-macro 0.85 on the test set. It has a Flask web app, Grad-CAM interpretability, an AUSLAN proposed vocabulary, and Darwin Core export for the Atlas of Living Australia. This was originally the foundation for an MPhil proposal at the University of Queensland.
>
> Project two is my master's thesis: the **Mexican raptors** project, scaled to 53 species. I benchmark four CNN architectures: MobileNetV3, EfficientNet-B3, ResNet-50 as baseline, and ConvNeXt-Tiny. Two novelties here. We train deliberately on the silhouette in flight, not on plumage, because that's what ornithologists actually use in the field. And we have a Bayesian behaviour module that fuses static image classification with flight-mode analysis from short video. The sign-language layer is in International Sign rather than AUSLAN.
>
> Project three is a **bioacoustics system** for passive acoustic monitoring of mammals, amphibians, reptiles and insects. PANNs-CNN14 pre-trained on AudioSet for the classifier, FastAPI for the REST service, PostgreSQL with PostGIS for spatial data, Docker for deployment, and CI/CD on GitHub Actions. Version 0.3 runs end-to-end.
>
> The doctoral synthesis - the question I want you to consider - is whether to do the **multimodal fusion** of these three pipelines as the doctoral thesis. Vision and audio classifiers vote together through Bayesian inference, the sign-language layer guarantees accessibility, the output feeds biodiversity infrastructure like GBIF and the Atlas of Living Australia.
>
> That's the picture. May I show you the details?"

**Word count:** ~510. **Speaking time:** ~3 minutes at 170 wpm. Slow, confident, articulated.

## Practice routine

**Monday-Thursday, 10 minutes per day:**

1. Read **Version 1** out loud, twice. Time it. Should land at 28-32 seconds.
2. Read **Version 2** out loud, once. Time it. Should land at 85-95 seconds.
3. Read one paragraph of **Version 3** out loud, slowly. Different paragraph each day.

**Thursday evening:**

Record yourself doing **Version 2** on your phone. Listen. If you stumble on a word, mark it in `Pronunciation_Guide_EN.md` and drill it.

**Friday morning before the meeting:**

Do **Version 1** twice in the car or on the bus. That's all. Trust the work you've done.

## Key phrases to never lose

These are the load-bearing words. If you forget everything else, these still carry the message:

- "Multi-modal AI for biodiversity identification."
- "Three projects, one research program."
- "Vision + audio + sign language."
- "Accessibility built in from day one."
- "F1-macro 0.85 on the Australian raptor test set."
- "53 species in Mexico, four architectures benchmarked."
- "Silhouette in flight, not plumage."
- "Bayesian behaviour fusion."
- "PANNs-CNN14 pre-trained on AudioSet."
- "FastAPI + PostgreSQL + Docker."
- "The doctorate is the multimodal fusion."

If you can stitch these together you will sound like you know exactly what you've built. Because you do.
